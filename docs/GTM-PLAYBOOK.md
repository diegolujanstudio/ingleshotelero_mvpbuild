## 1. THE SALES DIAGNOSIS — lead with turnover, not English

The buyer thinks they have a knowledge gap. They have a **turnover gap**, and English is where it hurts.

**The numbers to open with:**
- **30–80% annual turnover** in Mexican hotels and restaurants (CANIRAC, via Expansión)
- Measured in Guanajuato hotels: **48.72% annual** (Caldera González et al., 67 HR managers, 2019)
- **Only 4 of those 67 hotels had a formal recruitment process. Only 4 conducted induction training.**
- **Cancún scores 414 on the EF EPI** — one of the lowest in Mexico (national 440, Playa del Carmen 517, Monterrey 532)

**The opening line:**
> *"Con 50% de rotación anual, cada empleado nuevo llega sin inglés. ¿Cuánto tiempo tarda hoy en poder atender a un huésped?"*

That reframes the purchase from *training* (a cost center, cut first) to *time-to-competence* (an operations metric tied to guest experience and revenue).

**The metric we sell on: `time-to-competence`** — days from hire to handling the ~20 utterances the role actually needs. Average CEFR level is the wrong headline metric, because 50% turnover destroys a cohort average every year and makes us look like we failed. Time-to-competence *improves* with turnover, because we get faster at onboarding each new wave.

### The three-part reframe
| They believe | The truth | What we say |
|---|---|---|
| "My staff need English lessons" | It's a motivation gap, not a knowledge gap | *"Sus empleados ya saben inglés. Les da pena hablarlo."* |
| "Training takes staff off the floor" | Ours never does | *"Cero horas de aula. Cero horas de nómina."* |
| "They'll leave anyway" | Exactly — so buy induction speed, not head-count fluency | *"No compramos el nivel promedio. Compramos la velocidad con la que un nuevo llega a servir."* |

### The 30-second demo that closes
Play the audio. `beach` / `b*tch`. `sheet` / `sh*t`.

Then: *"Este es el sonido que hace que su personal deje de hablar inglés con los huéspedes. Lo arreglamos en la primera semana."*

No HR director forgets this. It converts an abstract benefit into a specific, visceral, operational problem they have definitely witnessed.

### The 2027 regulatory wedge
Mexico's **40-hour workweek reform phases in from 2027 to 2030.** Hotels must restructure 24/7 operations — more people, shorter shifts, less on-the-job absorption time, and a much harder case for pulling anyone into a classroom.

> *"En 2027 va a tener más personal, en turnos más cortos, con menos tiempo para aprender en el piso. Una práctica de cinco minutos por WhatsApp es el único formato que sobrevive a esa reestructura."*

This is a time-boxed wedge. Use it in 2026–2027 while it is urgent and unclaimed.

## 2. THE OFFER (Sovereign Architecture, Layer 2)

**Value equation:** `Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort)`

Our structural advantage is the **denominator**. Competitors require scheduling, classrooms, and payroll hours. We require five minutes on a phone the worker already owns, on a channel that costs them nothing.

| Variable | Our position |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Staff who speak to guests without fear; measurably better service; a property that certifies bilingual |
| Perceived likelihood | The placement exam produces a real number in week one. The pilot proves it in 14 days |
| Time delay | First measurable change in 14 days. Not a semester |
| **Effort** | **Zero classroom hours. Zero payroll hours. Zero IT work. Zero app install.** |

**Offer architecture (Layer 3):**
- **Attraction** — the free 14-day pilot, one department, up to 10 employees, no card
- **Core** — Profesional, $300 USD/property/month
- **Upsell at peak satisfaction** — additional properties; the padrino program; custom brand content
- **Continuity** — the subscription itself, defended by the certificate and the HR record
- **Downsell** — Inicial at $150, one department only

**Keep per-property pricing.** Per-seat annual commitments assume the same humans are present in 12 months. They will not be. Per-property survives churn and is the correct instrument for this market.

## 3. IMPLEMENTATION — the hotel does almost nothing

The single biggest objection is *"we don't have time to implement software."* The answer must be that there is nothing to implement.

**No install. No IT. No integration. No procurement cycle.** It is a link.

### The 20-minute rollout
| Step | Who | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. HR sends us the staff list (name, role, WhatsApp, shift) — a spreadsheet or a photo of the roster | HR | 5 min |
| 2. We generate one permanent access link per employee | Us | automatic |
| 3. Links delivered by WhatsApp, not email | Us | automatic |
| 4. Employee taps once. No password, no signup | Employee | 10 sec |
| 5. Placement exam, 30 min, whenever their shift allows — or a batch session in the break room | Employee | 30 min |
| 6. HR opens the dashboard and sees the property's real English baseline | HR | 5 min |

**Step 6 is the activation event for the buyer.** They have never seen this number before. Nobody has ever measured it. That is the moment the pilot converts.

### Two activation events, and both must fire
Product-Led Onboarding logic inverts for **mandated** software: the user did not choose to sign up, so social influence becomes the dominant adoption lever (UTAUT/Venkatesh). We therefore need:

1. **Buyer activation** — HR sees the baseline dashboard (day 1)
2. **Learner activation** — *"I said a real sentence to a real guest and they understood me"* (day 2–5)

Our activation clock is **"did they come back on day 2,"** not "did they finish onboarding." Benchmark reality: median SaaS activation is 25–30%; we should target far higher because the employer manufactures the initial intent — but we must *earn* day 2.

**The highest-leverage activation surface is the supervisor, not the app.** A manager who mentions it in the pre-shift briefing outperforms any notification we can send. Give them the script.

### What we hand HR on day one
- The **política de práctica** — a one-page PDF they attach to the *reglamento interior*, authorizing 5 minutes in the break room. This removes permission ambiguity, which is a real adoption blocker and costs us one document.
- The **pre-shift briefing script** — 30 seconds a supervisor reads out, including this week's "quién avanzó más."
- The **baseline report** — the property's English level, by role, on day one.

### Designed for churn
- Bulk enroll and offboard
- Seat reassignment within the month at no cost
- Reporting **per-role and per-cohort**, not per-named-individual — so a departure does not look like a failure
- A new hire is enrolled, placed, and drilling **within one shift**

> 63 of 67 hotels studied had no induction process at all. **We can be their induction process.** That is a larger product than English training, and it is the natural expansion path.

## 4. THE CERTIFICATE — the most underused asset we have

**There is no recognized national credential for hotel English in Mexico.** Mexico's competency standard for front-desk hotel work, **CONOCER/SEP EC0045**, contains *no English-language performance criteria*.

That is an open category.

**Issue a real certificate:** role-specific, CEFR-anchored, carrying the hotel's name and ours, with a verification URL and a date.

Why it matters more than any badge:
- It is **portable career capital the learner personally owns**. Duolingo cannot give a bellboy something to put on a job application. We can.
- It motivates precisely the person we need to motivate. Riviera Maya hospitality workers earn **25.8% below the national average**, and **64.7% migrated states for economic advancement**. This workforce is not change-resistant — it is resistant to effort with an *unclear payoff*. The certificate makes the payoff legible.
- Frame it on the ladder the worker actually sees: *"esto vale para tu siguiente puesto"* — recepción pays more than botones; a chain property pays more than a local hotel.
- It gives the hotel a marketing asset: **"Propiedad Bilingüe Certificada."**

**Strategic move worth serious evaluation: pursue CONOCER alignment or become an Entidad de Certificación.** A SEP-recognized credential for hotel English would be a durable moat and a procurement unlock, and it is currently unowned.

*Caution: do not claim H-2B or J-1 visa outcomes in marketing. We could not verify a published English requirement for H-2B, and volumes for Mexico are unconfirmed. Say what is true; the career ladder argument is strong enough without it.*

## 5. CONTENT AND UPDATE ECONOMICS

Diego's open question: *what do we ship, how often, and what happens when a learner finishes everything?*

### The answer: we are not a content company
A content company must keep authoring or it dies. A **system company** generates content from the installed base.

Three engines, in order of leverage:

**1. CAPTURA (zero marginal authoring cost).**
Every learner, every day, answers *"¿Qué no supiste decir hoy?"* Those become curated situations with attribution. The hotel generates our curriculum. Ten properties × 40 employees × one capture per day is **12,000 real hospitality-English failures per month**, mapped by role and market. No competitor can obtain this.

**2. Combinatorial generation.**
`situation × guest personality × pressure × valid solution paths`. Forty authored situations become effectively unbounded. This is Core Drive 3 — the mechanism behind games that last decades.

**3. Authored core (the only part with real cost).**
"Las Primeras 400" per role, the minimal-pair inventory, and the exam bank. **This is a one-time build per role, not a treadmill.** The phoneme inventory in particular is fixed by linguistics and never needs updating.

### Cadence
| Cadence | What ships | Cost to us |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuous** | CAPTURA-sourced situations entering the library | ~0 |
| **Monthly** | Curated "situaciones nuevas" drop + the property's monthly assessment | Low, mostly curation |
| **Quarterly** | A new role module or a seasonal set (Semana Santa, temporada alta, Día de Muertos) | Medium |
| **Annual** | A new language or a new sector | Large, strategic |

Monthly is the right public cadence: frequent enough to justify a subscription, slow enough to be sustainable for a two-person company.

### Add-ons (real upsells, not invented ones)
- **Contenido de marca** — the chain's own service standards and scripts drilled verbatim. High value, high price, and it deepens switching costs enormously
- **Padrino program** — mentor tooling and reporting
- **Segundo idioma** — French/German/Portuguese for luxury properties. The method transfers; only the phoneme table and frequency list change
- **Certificación de propiedad** — the audit and the "Propiedad Bilingüe" mark
- **Módulos nuevos** — housekeeping, spa, seguridad, mantenimiento. Same method, new roles

### When a learner finishes everything
They do not finish. See `METODO-TURNO.md` §8:
1. The library is combinatorial, not enumerated
2. Their own shift keeps generating new situations
3. At B1+ they become a **padrino/madrina** — the ladder switches from *level* to *scope*
4. A second language opens

**Graduation is a promotion, not an exit.**

## 6. THE SOVEREIGN ARCHITECTURE AUDIT

Diego's framework applied to Inglés Hotelero. *Find the weakest layer and repair it before spending anywhere else.*

| Layer | State | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **0 · Operator's OS** | WHY is clear and genuine: dignity for frontline workers. Hedgehog is well-defined | **Strong** |
| **1 · Market** | Situation niche is sharp. **Category claim now available: "el padre lingüístico, no el maestro"** and "arreglamos los sonidos que dan pena." Cancún's 414 EPI is the starving-crowd proof | **Strong, newly sharpened** |
| **2 · Offer** | Value equation dominates on the denominator (zero classroom hours). Certificate is an under-exploited gain creator | **Good** |
| **3 · Money model** | Per-property pricing is correct for a 50%-turnover market. WhatsApp unit cost ~$0.24/learner/month against $150–500/property is excellent | **Good** |
| **4 · Pipeline** | **7 leads, all uncontacted, some 6+ weeks old. Zero warm outreach happening.** | **⚠ WEAKEST LAYER** |
| **5 · Conversion** | The 30-second beach/sheet demo is a strong System-1 opener. Untested at volume | **Unproven** |
| **6 · Flywheel** | Padrino + CAPTURA + certificate are genuine compounding loops — but **zero customers means zero flywheel** | **Designed, not running** |
| **7 · AI & leverage** | Strong. AI is the coach, the assessor, the content generator, and the curator | **Strong** |

### The verdict, stated plainly

**Layer 4 is the weakest layer and it is not close.** The product thesis is now unusually well-founded; the pipeline is empty. Every hour spent on Layers 0–3 and 5–7 before Layer 4 is repaired violates the framework's own instruction.

The Decision Engine's Must-Have threshold (Ellis, 40%+ "very disappointed") **cannot be measured at zero customers.**

**Coherent action:** the P0 build list in `METODO-TURNO.md` §11 exists to make the *first three pilots* land and prove. It is not a reason to delay them. Build the minimal-pair trainer and the Coach/Evaluator split — those two are days of work and they are the demo — then call the seven leads.
